How the Website Is Doing on Google

A plain-English read of three months of Google data for reaganevents.com: what the numbers mean, what is working, where the gaps are, and what we do about it.

This pairs with your Website Strategy page. That page covers the three changes. This one shows the evidence behind them.

The Short Version

  • Traffic is steady, not growing.Google sends the site about 12 visitors a day, and that number held flat all summer. Nothing is broken. Nothing is climbing either.

  • The journal is the engine.Six out of every ten visits from Google land on a journal article, not the homepage. The venue guides for The Dunlin and The Cooper are the best performers on the whole site.

  • People who know your name find you easily.Someone searching "Reagan Events" sees you near the top of the page, and four in ten of them click.

  • People who don't know you yet mostly don't find you.The site shows up a lot for broad searches, but usually on page two, where very few people look. Italy and your event services are the clearest examples.

Reading the Numbers

Google Search Console reports four numbers. Here is what each one means in everyday terms, and how the site measures up.

66,434

Times the site showed up

Healthy

How many times any page from your site appeared in someone's Google results. They did not have to click. They just had to see it.

To improve it: keep publishing journal guides to specific venues and destinations you know well. Each one gives Google another search to show you for, without adding pages to your main menu.

Google calls this "impressions."

How many times someone clicked through to your site from Google. These are the people who walked in the door.

To improve it: lift the two numbers below. More people clicking, and pages moving higher in the list, both turn into more visits.

Google calls this "clicks."

1.6%

Of people clicked

Worth improving

Out of everyone who saw your site in Google, the share who clicked. For every 100 people who walked past the window, about 2 came in.

To improve it: rewrite the page titles and short descriptions Google shows, starting with the Santa Barbara guide. The fix list is under Fixing It in Squarespace. Lead with the words people actually search, like the venue or city name.

What This Tells Us

Your Name Is Doing Its Job

When someone already knows Reagan Events, Google puts you right at the top and nearly half of them click.

Venue Guides Bring the Right People

Searches for a specific venue, like "the dunlin wedding," are small in number but reach exactly the right audience, and they click at a healthy rate.

The Biggest Numbers Are Not Always the Best Visitors

The Santa Barbara and Taylor Swift posts account for nearly a third of all the times the site showed up, but most of those people are browsing, not hiring a planner.

The Searches That Lead to Clients Are on Page Two or Lower

Searches most likely to come from a future client, like "wedding planners in charleston," "brand events," and anything about Italy, currently land between spots 16 and 28, where almost no one sees them.

People Are Searching in Full Sentences

Nearly 90 long, detailed searches found the site but none were clicked, and pages that answer a clear question in a clear sentence, like the new FAQ page, tend to win these.

Where the Gaps Are

Brand, Corporate, and Private Events Are Hard to Find

You want brand and corporate work to reach about a quarter of your events, but those pages, together with private events, brought in 12 of 1,079 visits this summer, about 1%.

The search "brand events" alone showed the site 719 times with no visits, and the brand event production page usually sits around spot 21. This is the biggest gap between where the business is going and what Google sees today.

A few topics have two nearly identical posts. When that happens, Google splits its attention between them and neither ranks as well as one strong post would.

Outside Recognition Is the Missing Piece

Google and AI tools like ChatGPT both lean on what others say about a business, and that is where Reagan Events has the most room to grow. Renewed press submissions, a refreshed Press page, and more visible links to the podcast and Substack all give them that proof.

Fixing It in Squarespace

Everything in this section happens inside Squarespace's regular settings. No code, and nothing on the page itself changes. Only what Google shows changes.

Two words to know. The SEO title is the blue headline people click in Google. The SEO description is the short gray summary under it. Squarespace has a field for each one on every page and every journal post.

Step by Step

Squarespace moves its menus around from time to time. If something below looks different, search the setting name at support.squarespace.com.

Stop the Doubled Brand Name

  1. Open the site-wide search settingsIn Squarespace, click Marketing, then SEO Appearance.
  2. Find the setting for journal postsOpen the Collection Items tab. Journal posts and podcast episodes count as collection items. The title format box probably reads %i — %s, which means "post title, then site name."
  3. Change it and saveReplace what is in the box with %i on its own, then click Save. Google will now show each post's own SEO title exactly as written.
  4. Check one postFrom now on, a post's SEO title only includes "Reagan Events" if you type it in. Most posts already end with "| Reagan Events," so nothing else needs to change.

Change a Journal Post's Title or Description

  1. Open the journalClick Pages, then click the journal. You will see the list of posts.
  2. Open the post settingsHover over the post, click the ... menu, and choose Settings.
  3. Edit the SEO tabClick SEO. Paste the new text into SEO Title and SEO Description. The preview shows roughly how it will look in Google.
  4. Save, and leave the URL aloneClick Save. Never change the post URL field. That address is what Google already knows.

Change a Regular Page's Title or Description

  1. Open the page settingsClick Pages. Hover over the page and click the gear icon.
  2. Edit the SEO tabClick SEO and fill in SEO Title and SEO Description.
  3. SaveClick Save. As with posts, do not touch the URL.

Combine a Duplicate Post

  1. Pick the one to keepKeep the post with more visits: the "anchor" Cooper guide, the "sophisticated coastal celebrations" Dunlin guide, the "power of a preferred vendor list" post, and the "diverse wedding venues" Santa Barbara guide.
  2. Move anything useful overIf the other post has a paragraph or photo the keeper is missing, copy it into the keeper first.
  3. Unpublish the extra postOpen the extra post's settings and switch it from Published to Draft. Do not delete it.
  4. Send its visitors to the keeperClick Settings, then Developer Tools, then URL Mappings. Add one line: the old address, an arrow, the new address, and 301. The Cooper line looks like this:/wedding-planning-journal/the-cooper-wedding-guide-charlestons-new-waterfront-address -> /wedding-planning-journal/the-cooper-wedding-guide-charlestons-new-waterfront-anchor 301Never edit or remove lines that are already there. Click Save.
  5. Test itOpen the old address in a private browser window. It should land on the post you kept.

Link From the Journal

  1. Open a popular postStart with The Dunlin, The Cooper, and the Kiawah guides, since those get the most visits.
  2. Find a natural sentenceLook for a line that mentions events, design, hosting, or Italy. Click Edit and highlight a few words in that sentence, like "brand events" or "weddings in Italy."
  3. Add the linkClick the link icon in the text toolbar, choose the matching page from the list, and click Save. One or two links per post is plenty.

When you finish a batch, send Lindsay the list of pages you changed. She will ask Google to look at them again through Search Console, and the new titles usually show up within two to four weeks.

A Monthly Ten-Minute Check

Brooke can run this once a month in Google Search Console, under Performance, set to the last 28 days.

  • Are weekly visits still in the 65 to 100 range, or higher? A drop below 50 for two weeks in a row is worth a closer look.
  • Is the average spot holding around 14 or better?
  • Do The Dunlin and The Cooper guides still have the most visits of any journal post?
  • After a title rewrite, has that page's click rate gone up? Allow two to four weeks.
  • Has anything new shown up in the search list that you did not expect? New searches are often ideas for the next journal post.
